# Campaign finance survey: what DataSF holds and how money ties to each official

**Kind of hop: a survey** of an outside source (the San Francisco Ethics Commission's campaign finance data on DataSF, https://data.sf.gov). These are findings, not a design. Numbers below come from live queries run on 2026-10-28 against data loaded through 2026-10-28; they are regenerated by `profile.py` into `data-profile.md` / `data-profile.json` in this directory. Where a number is quoted here, the table that produced it is in `data-profile.md`.

## Summary

- DataSF publishes **four daily-refreshed core datasets** from the Ethics Commission (Transactions, Filings, Summary Totals, Filers), joined by `-nid` keys, plus smaller as-needed datasets (public funds approved, individual expenditure ceilings) and two stale election-window extracts (see 1.2).
- **Nothing in the data says "this money belongs to Supervisor X".** Records are tied to *committees* (`filer_nid`); committees are tied to people only by a free-text `candidate_name` on the Filers dataset, with no office, district or term. Independent spending names candidates only on some forms, with typos and inconsistent office codes. Linking is a matching job that needs a reviewed roster.
- **Completeness is good for the committees that exist, and exact on one check:** for every committee tied to the Mayor and 11 supervisors, itemized + unitemized Schedule A in Transactions equals the Form 460 summary Schedule A total to the dollar (gap $0 for all 54 matched committees). The gaps are elsewhere: paper filings carry no transactions and are 49% of all filing rows (22% of current filings since 2012); several sitting officials' committees are terminated, so recent giving to them is not in the data; and officeholder/federal/state activity may sit outside DataSF.
- **Double counting is the main fairness trap.** Form 497 (24-hour reports) and Form 496 / Schedule D / Form 461 report money that is also on Schedule A or on each other. Summing all transaction rows for a committee overstates it.
- **Amendments replace, they do not append.** An amended filing keeps its `filing_nid`, gets a new `filing_activity_nid` and `filing_id_number`, and the earlier version's transactions are deleted from the Transactions dataset. A quarter of recent Form 460 reports have been amended, sometimes years later.
- **There is no donor identifier.** Donors are free-text names; only committee donors carry an FPPC id, and 1,034 of the 1,156 state-style ids found in neither SF dataset resolve in California's own committee records (the state's bulk export). The same company appears under up to ten spellings.

## 1. Datasets and record shape (criterion 1)

### 1.3 Transactions: what a record holds (102 columns)

Who gave: `transaction_first_name`, `transaction_last_name` (organisations put their whole name in last name), `transaction_city/state/zip`, `transaction_employer`, `transaction_occupation`, `transaction_self`, `entity_code` (IND individual, COM committee, OTH other e.g. business or union, SCC small-contributor committee, PTY party), `committee_id` (FPPC id when the giver is a committee), `intermediary_*` for bundlers/agents.

How much and when: `transaction_amount_1` (this item), `transaction_amount_2` (cumulative calendar year), `calculated_amount` and `calculated_date` (the dataset's "most appropriate" amount/date, which is the field to use for totals), `transaction_date`, loan and accrued-expense amounts.

To which committee: `filer_nid` (stable id), `fppc_id` (state id; can be "pending"), `filer_name` and `filer_type` *as of the filing*. Filing context: `filing_id_number`, `filing_nid`, `filing_activity_nid`, `filing_type` (FiledOriginal / FiledAmendment), `filing_version`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `filing_date`.

What kind of line: `form_type` is a Form 460 schedule letter (A monetary contributions received, B1 loans received, C non-monetary, D expenditures supporting/opposing others, E payments, F unpaid bills, G agent payments, H loans made, I misc increases to cash) or a form code (`F497P1` contributions received in a 24-hour report, `F497P2` contributions made, `F496` independent expenditure report, `F496P3` contributors to that report, `F461P5` expenditures on Form 461, `F465P3`). Unitemized totals appear as extra rows (`F460ALine2` etc., one per filing).

Whom spending is about: `candidate_first_name/last_name`, `office_code`, `office_sought_held`, `district_number`, `support_oppose_code` (S/O), `ballot_name/number/jurisdiction`. Populated only on Schedule D, F496 and F461P5 rows; Schedule A rows never carry a candidate.

Housekeeping: `data_as_of`, `data_loaded_at`, `element_nid` (row key, not stable across amendments), `external_id`, `cross_reference_*`.

Row volumes by form (full table in `data-profile.md` section 2): Schedule A 495,960 rows / $523.5M; E 186,416; F 48,789; G 37,051; D 26,639; F497P1 10,458 rows / $228.9M; F496 7,171; B1 loans 6,293. Filing volume: 27,557 distinct filings from 2,547 filers have transactions; transaction filing dates run 1998-01-27 to 2026-10-27.

### 1.4 Filers, Filings and Summary records

Filers: `filer_nid` (primary key), `fppc_id`, current `filer_name`, `filer_type`, `status` (ACTIVE / TERMINATED / ANNUAL / NR / NONSF), `is_terminated`, city/state/zip, `candidate_name` ("Last, First", present for 2,000+ filers). Types include Candidate or Officeholder (1,712 terminated, 107 active), Primarily Formed Candidate, Primarily Formed Measure, General Purpose, Independent Expenditure, Major Donor, Slate Mailer, Person, "470 [Officeholder]".

Filings: form (FPPC 410, 460, 461, 496, 497, 470, 501, SFEC 161-163, ...), filing and period dates, `filing_type`, `filing_version`, `mechanism_type` (Electronic/Paper), `has_efile_content`, `rendering_url` (PDF), `current_status` ("Most Recent (synched date)" or "Superseded by <activity id>"), `rejected`, `is_termination`.

Summary: per filing, the numbered summary lines of Form 460 (e.g. `line_5_col_a` total contributions this period, `schedulea_line_1/2/3` itemized / unitemized / total monetary contributions, ending cash `line_16_col_a`, outstanding debts), with `sync_flag`.

### 1.5 Data quality seen in the records

- 251 transaction rows have a date before 1998 or in the future (years 1900 and 2203, one 2033); filers mistyped. Use `filing_date`/`end_date` as a cross-check and drop out-of-range `calculated_date` values.
- 3,692 Schedule A rows are negative (refunds/returns), summing -$3.66M; totals must include them.
- Transactions have no blank `filing_id_number` today, though the dataset notes say a nightly copy step can briefly blank it; join on `filing_activity_nid` if it happens.
- Contributor fields: individuals have employer on 94% and occupation on 99% of itemized rows; committees and organisations almost never do; first name is absent for organisations by design.

## 2. How complete the data is for the current officials (criterion 2)

Roster used (Mayor and 11 supervisors; sources in Open questions): Mayor Daniel Lurie; Supervisors Connie Chan (D1), Stephen Sherrill (D2), Danny Sauter (D3), Alan Wong (D4), Bilal Mahmood (D5), Matt Dorsey (D6), Myrna Melgar (D7), Rafael Mandelman (D8), Jackie Fielder (D9), Shamann Walton (D10), Chyanne Chen (D11). Per-committee tables for each are in `data-profile.md` section 5.

### 2.1 Period covered

- Transactions start in **1998** (first filing 1998-01-27); 1998-2026 Schedule A has between roughly 5,500 and 52,000 rows a year, peaking in election years (2024: 52,385 rows, $60.0M). The Filings dataset reaches back to 1976 for registration forms but those have no transaction data.
- For the sitting officials the usable history is their **committees' own lifetimes**, not their time in office: earliest Schedule A row for a current official's own Supervisor/Mayor committee ranges from 2009 (Mandelman's 2010 race) through 2020-2025 for most; Lurie's first row is 2023-09-26 (before he held office).
- Everything is current to the previous night for active committees (last transaction dates 2026-10-16/17 for Sherrill, Wong and Dorsey's 2026 committees).

Reading the table:

1. **Money to a sitting official appears only while their committee is open and e-filing.** Officials with terminated committees (Chan, Melgar, Mandelman, Walton, Chen) show no recent contributions, which means "none reported to a committee we know of", not "none". Any new committee (for example an officeholder or re-election account opened under a new name) must be discovered by re-running the filer match.
2. **Reconciliation is exact.** For each of the 54 matched committees, `Schedule A (A rows) + unitemized (F460ALine2) = Summary Totals schedulea_line_3` summed over current Form 460s (gap $0). Within DataSF the two datasets tell the same story, so a mismatch in later polling signals a sync problem (the Summary dataset has a `sync_flag`; an unset flag means a paper amendment replaced an electronic filing).
3. **Paper filings are invisible in Transactions.** Matched committees together have 164 current paper filings against 435 electronic filings (forms 460, 461, 450, 470 and 410 only); this survey did not break paper filings down by form, so it cannot say how many paper filings are contribution reports (unverified). Across the dataset, paper filings were 49% of filing rows overall but 22% of current filings since 2012 and 5-8% in 2024-2026.
4. **Public financing is a separate source.** `dbak-p2fq` lists approved public funds per candidate (for example Sherrill $252,000 for the 2026-06-02 election, Wong $252,000). It is not in Transactions as a contribution; show it separately.
5. **Federal and state-level activity is not in DataSF.** The Filers dataset only holds committees registered with the SF Ethics Commission. A supervisor's congressional, state legislative or ballot-measure activity at the state level would be on FEC / California Secretary of State systems. Fielder's 2022 State Senate run appears only through other committees' Schedule D lines. The survey did not query those systems.

## 3. How money ties to an official (criterion 3)

There is **no official id, office, district or term in any of the four core datasets.** Ties are built from the pieces below.

### 3.1 Committees (strongest tie)

- Filers dataset `candidate_name` ("Last, First", sometimes "Last, Legal Name (Preferred)") links a committee to a person. `filer_type` says what the committee is:
  - *Candidate or Officeholder*: the official's own controlled committee. The **name** carries the office and year ("for Supervisor 2024", "for Democratic County Central Committee", "College Board"), so which office a committee is for is a text judgement, not a field.
  - *Primarily Formed Candidate*: a third-party committee formed to support **or oppose** the named candidate. Example: "Grow SF PAC Opposing Chan for Supervisor 2024" has `candidate_name` = Chan. Support vs oppose is only in the committee's name (and its own Schedule D lines). Treat as not the official's money.
  - *Primarily Formed Measure*: ballot-measure committees. `candidate_name` is blank; officials' own measure committees ("Mayor Lurie's Yes on H Committee") are found only by name search. They are controlled by the official but are measure money.
  - *470 [Officeholder]*, *Person*, *General Purpose*, *Major Donor*: need individual review.
- Contributions to a committee are Schedule A rows with that `filer_nid`. Loans (B1), non-monetary (C) and miscellaneous cash increases (I) are separate schedules and should be shown separately, not folded into "contributions".
- A person has many committees over time (matched 1 to 13 per official here), including those for other offices. A fair "money beside the official" figure must name the committee, office and cycle it covers.

### 3.2 Independent spending

- Independent expenditures name candidates on **Form 496** rows (`F496`), **Form 461 part 5** (`F461P5`) and **Schedule D** of a 460 (`D`): fields `candidate_first_name/last_name`, `office_code`, `support_oppose_code`, `ballot_*`. Ties are by name; a payer is the row's `filer_nid`.
- Name hygiene: filed first names have typos ("Conne", "Lawence"); `office_code` is inconsistent for the same race (BSU, CSU, OTH, blank all occur for supervisor races, MAY or blank for Mayor); only last name plus first-name prefix plus manual review is safe. A different "Doug Chan" or "Queena Chen" would match on surname alone.
- Rows from different forms report the same money. Test over 2018 onward: of 263 payer/candidate/stance combinations present on both F496 and Schedule D, 108 match to within $1; payer-candidate pairs that appear on only one of the two forms are 102 (F496) and 1,253 (Schedule D). Dorsey's opposition spending is $37,977 on both forms. **Never add forms together; choose one form per payer or reconcile.**
- 24-hour reports (`F497P1`, contributions received) are not additive with Schedule A: since 2022, 2,865 of 4,470 `F497P1` rows ($72.0M of $83.2M) have a matching Schedule A row (same committee, date, amount, name). Lurie's committee: $7.93M of F497P1 against $11.7M on Schedule A in total.
- Examples of independent spending beside officials (name match, support/oppose as filed, F496 unless noted): Lurie support $3.74M (F496, MAY), oppose $1.60M (+$1.39M with no office code); Walton support $107K; Dorsey oppose $38K; Sauter oppose $56K; Mahmood oppose $66K; Chen support $45K plus $38K with no office code. Supporting and opposing amounts are about a candidate in an election, not a gift to the official.

### 3.3 What to say beside an amount

The data supports statements of the form "$X reported by committee Y (a candidate committee / a committee supporting / opposing / an independent spender) in filings through DATE". It does not support "contributions to Supervisor X" without naming the committee type, and it says nothing about intent or effect. That matches the project's non-goal of implying causation.

## 4. Update frequency and amended filings (criterion 4)

### 4.1 Cadence

- The four core datasets say they refresh **nightly from midnight Pacific**; each warns that viewing during the run (until about 8am) may show incomplete data. All four show `rowsUpdatedAt` = 2026-10-28 and max `data_loaded_at` 2026-10-28.
- Observed loads: rows loaded per day in the last two weeks ranged from 8 to 5,764 (2026-10-23, 96 filings) and arrive on weekends too (2026-10-25: 8 rows). Filings received per day ranged from 1 to 106 in the same fortnight, heavier near the 2026-11-03 election.
- Reporting lag matters more than refresh lag. For Schedule A rows dated 2024 or later: 1,206 of 73,867 were filed within 7 days of the transaction, 7,572 within 30, 34,230 within 90, 51,827 within 180. Semi-annual and quarterly 460s dominate; pre-election weeks add 24-hour reports (Form 497) and Form 496.
- The public funds datasets refresh three times a day (Public Funds Approved) or as needed (IEC); the election-window extracts (`s9b2-abj2`, `3n88-2rrb`) are stale since 2024/2025.
- Polling more often than daily gains nothing from the core datasets; a daily poll after about 8am Pacific, with a read of the latest `data_loaded_at`, is consistent with the publisher's description (this is a reading of the publisher's text, not a decision).

### 4.2 How amendments appear

Taken from the dataset descriptions and checked on the Mayor's committee and across all Form 460 reports first filed since 2022:

- A **report period has a stable `filing_nid`**. Every submission of it (original, amendment) is a new `filing_activity_nid` with its **own `filing_id_number`** and a `filing_version` (1 original, 2 first amendment, ...). Example: the Mayor's 2023 annual report was filed 2024-01-31 and superseded by version 4 on 2024-08-30.
- **Filings dataset** keeps superseded submissions: 13,128 rows say "Superseded by <activity id>", 60,726 say "Most Recent (synched date)" (Filings: 76,423 rows; 61,103 distinct `filing_nid`).
- **Transactions and Summary Totals hold only the most recent submission.** An amendment "completely replaces the original and any prior amendments"; the earlier rows are removed. In Transactions 405,236 of 983,387 rows come from amendments (v2: 241,926; v3: 85,041; v4: 44,239; up to v11).
- Consequences: transaction `element_nid` and `transaction_id` do not persist across amendments (the dataset says they are only unique within a filing), so rows cannot be matched one-to-one before and after; **there is no change history** in the data. To stay correct, a consumer must replace all rows for a `filing_nid` whenever its `filing_activity_nid` changes.
- Amendments are common and can be late. Reports are grouped by `filing_nid` and by the date of their first submission, and only `FiledAmendment` submissions (rejected ones left out) count as amendments. Of 3,738 Form 460 reports first filed since 2022, 1,013 (27%) have at least one amendment (1 amendment: 677 reports, 2: 211, 3: 76, 4: 36, up to 7). Between the first submission and the last amendment the median is 87 days, the 90th percentile 350 days and the maximum 1,587 days; 91 amended reports were last amended more than a year after the first filing. One of the 1,013 has a paper amendment. Amendments filed since 2022 to reports first filed earlier are not in these figures. Totals shown for older periods can still move.
- **Rejected** filings (about 164 filings in the Filings dataset) and their transactions are excluded from Transactions. A paper amendment of an electronic filing leaves `sync_flag` blank in Summary Totals until reconciled (the Commission says it is still working on that).
- "Contents changed" and "attachments added" submissions appear in Filings (2,032 rows across all forms) with no new transaction data; they are not counted as amendments above. Among Form 460 reports first filed since 2022, none has such a submission without also having an amendment.

## 5. Recognising the same donor (criterion 5)

### 5.1 What identifies a giver

- **No donor id exists.** Schedule A carries a name, city, state, zip, employer and occupation as typed by each committee.
- **Committee donors (`entity_code` COM, SCC, PTY) carry `committee_id`** (an FPPC id) on 13,238 of 13,707 COM rows; 469 COM rows (about $23.5M) have none. Of 2,654 distinct committee ids on Schedule A, 1,143 are in the SF Filers dataset (`fppc_id`), 96 more appear only in the State Filer extract, and 1,415 are in neither; section 5.3 looks those 1,415 up in California's committee records. 1,735 Filers rows have a pending, unavailable or n/a `fppc_id`.
- **Organisations (`OTH`) have no id at all:** the name is the only key, and employer/occupation are blank.
- **Individuals** have first name, last name, zip, employer and occupation, but no id.

### 5.2 How much the same giver varies

- Organisation and committee names on Schedule A: 30,669 distinct spellings; 23,600 after lowercasing, removing punctuation, parenthetical notes and corporate suffixes; 23,046 if PAC/committee words are also dropped (the second merge is a judgement, since a company and its PAC are legally distinct givers). 4,238 normalized names still have several spellings, covering $203M of $309M.
- Examples (spellings after normalization, dollars): Pacific Gas and Electric 10 spellings, $14.5M; Salesforce 7, $6.1M; Committee on Jobs Government Reform Fund 7, $4.1M; Airbnb 4, $9.5M; San Francisco Giants 3, $3.8M. Spellings seen include all-caps variants, "&" vs "and", "Company" vs "Corporation", parenthetical responsible-officer notes ("Google LLC and Affiliated Entities(Responsible Officer: ...)") and committee names that embed the sponsor ("Committee to Expand the Middle Class, Supported by Airbnb, Inc."), where whether it is the same giver is a policy decision.
- Individuals since 2018 (`data-profile.md` section 7, `people`): 74,905 distinct raw (last, first, zip) combinations collapse to 62,682 normalised name + 5-digit-zip keys after lowercasing, dropping punctuation and keeping the first token of the first name; there are 55,734 distinct normalised names, and 5,551 of them appear under more than one 5-digit zip (movers or different people). Without an id, grouping individuals can both split one person and merge two, so individual donors should be shown with their committee, amount and date, and any total flagged as name-based.

### 5.3 Gaps filled from outside DataSF

**The 1,415 committee ids found in neither SF dataset, looked up in California's committee records.** The state publishes its whole CAL-ACCESS database as one bulk export (https://campaignfinance.cdn.sos.ca.gov/dbwebexport.zip, last modified 2026-10-28). Its filer-name table (`FILERNAME_CD.TSV`: filer id, legacy cross-reference id, filer type, status, effective date, name, address; about 350,000 filers) was joined to every one of the 1,415 ids, not a sample (`data-profile.md` section 7, `unresolved_in_state_records`):

| What the id looks like | Ids | Schedule A dollars |
|---|---|---|
| 6 or 7 digits (state / FPPC style) | 1,156 | $29.5M |
| `C` + 8 digits (federal, FEC style) | 83 | $0.3M |
| Anything else (Pending, N, requested, none, SFO-prefixed numbers, payment-app names) | 176 | $2.1M |

- **1,034 of the 1,156 state-style ids resolve** ($29.25M of $29.51M): 619 as a state filer id and 415 as a legacy cross-reference id; 122 are not in the table. By state filer type: 956 recipient committees, 27 major-donor / independent-expenditure committees, 21 treasurer/responsible-officer records, 13 lobbying clients, 8 slate-mailer organisations, 6 candidate/officeholder, 3 payment-to-influence. By status: 544 terminated, 489 active, 1 inactive. The 37 records that are not committee types (treasurer, client, payment-to-influence) may be id collisions or filers with several roles; this was not checked (unverified).
- **What a resolved id gives:** a name, a filer type and a status. The largest unresolved dollars resolve to organisations that also appear as donor names in DataSF: American Beverage Association California PAC ($9.2M under id 1344506), "A More Affordable California, Sponsored by Uber" ($9.2M), Issues Mobilization PAC ($2.6M), AT&T California Employee PAC ($0.3M). Only equality of ids was tested; agreement between the state name and the donor name written in DataSF was read for these largest ones, not tested for all (unverified) (Peradapt could not verify this: matched ids never checked against names).
- **The 83 federal-style ids** and the **176 other values** cannot be looked up in this table. Federal committees would be on FEC records, which were not queried. Placeholders such as Pending cannot be resolved by any outside record; the donor's name is the only key there.
- **Company parents and sponsors.** The state table has no parent or sponsor column. 42 of the resolved state names contain the words "sponsored by" (for example a ballot-measure committee sponsored by a named company), so a sponsor is recoverable only as text in the committee name. The state's filer-link table (`FILER_LINKS_CD.TSV`) exists in the same export and its link types were not examined. No corporate-ownership source (company parents, subsidiaries) was surveyed, and none is implied by DataSF or by the state table.

**Donor priorities** (what the donor wants, with citations) are not in campaign finance data at all; the project assumes they come from the donor's own published material.

## Open questions

### What was looked for and not found
- **Any field tying a committee to an office, district or term.** Looked in the column lists and descriptions of the four core datasets and the Data Key. Absent: office must come from `filer_name` text or Schedule D/F496 `office_code`.
- **Any history of amended values.** Looked in Transactions, Filings and Summary: only the latest submission's rows exist.
- **A published refresh log or a schedule beyond "nightly".** Only descriptive text and `rowsUpdatedAt` / `data_loaded_at` were used.
- **Federal (FEC) records, and California state filings by or for the officials.** Not queried; not on DataSF. Only the state's filer-name table (committee identities) was read.
- **Which paper filings are contribution reports.** Paper filings were counted but not split by form for each committee.

### How to regenerate
`python profile.py` (from this directory; it queries DataSF anonymously, with retries on throttling, and reads the state's filer-name table from the bulk export by byte ranges) rewrites `data-profile.md` and `data-profile.json`. `python -m unittest discover -s tests` runs the helper tests.
