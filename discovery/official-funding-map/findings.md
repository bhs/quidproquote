# Official funding map: which donors with stated priorities gave through committees tied to the Mayor and the eleven supervisors, and where each worked pairing now stands

**Kind of hop: a survey** of sources outside the project (the Ethics Commission's campaign finance Transactions and Filers datasets on DataSF, the Commission's published contribution rules, donors' own pages, and the city's caption transcripts), written as **findings**. It makes no decisions. It starts from the money, which the earlier worked pairings (`../worked-pairing-examples/findings.md`) did not: they started from matched words and found money for one of six pairs, and that one was outside spending, not a gift.

The map itself is `funding-map.md` (and `funding-map.json`), the five pairings are `pairings.md`, and each is also a self-contained card (`pairing-cards.md`, styled in `pairing-cards.html`, as data in `pairing-cards.json`; the cards are shown below under "Pairing cards"); all are written by `python build_map.py` from the snapshot in `data/`, which `python collect.py` refreshes (network needed). This file says what they show and how far to trust it. Money was retrieved 2026-10-30, data loaded through 2026-10-30 (`data/money_snapshot.json`).

## Summary by charter question

1. **Which committees are tied to each official?** 55 committees for the twelve officials (Mayor Lurie and Supervisors Chan, Sherrill, Sauter, Wong, Mahmood, Dorsey, Melgar, Mandelman, Fielder, Walton and Chen), listed one table per official in `funding-map.md` section 1 with relation, status, Schedule A rows and total, 24-hour-only total and last filing. Only the **Mayor** has committees "for a ballot measure" carrying his name: three (`Mayor Lurie's Yes on D, E & F Committee`, `Mayor Lurie's Yes on H Committee`, `Daniel Lurie for Prop E`, the last terminated), holding $18.6M of Schedule A between them. **No supervisor has a ballot-measure committee carrying their name** in either dataset (a search of every committee name for each surname, and of all active measure committees, found none). The tie for a measure committee is its name, and control is inferred, not read from a filing: no committee's statement of organization (Form 410) was read, and the datasets have no field for who controls a committee. The inference rests on the state's committee-name rule (a committee a candidate controls must carry the candidate's last name, 2 CCR section 18402(c)(1)), on the Ethics Commission's dashboards listing the Mayor's two measure committees as "Mayor Lurie's Ballot Measure Committee", and on press calling them the mayor's committees (see "Where the sources fall short"). Supervisors' committees are mostly their own candidate and officeholder committees for supervisor, plus committees for other offices (Democratic County Central Committee, College Board, School Board), and a few primarily formed committees supporting or opposing them, which are not the official's own and are shown apart.
2. **Who gave, and how is an organisation's own committee recognised?** Section 2 lists the largest organisation and individual givers per official, section 3 the recognition. A committee giver row carries an FPPC id in 562 of 568 cases; 338 of those ids resolve to a committee registered with the Ethics Commission, whose registered name states the organisation or its sponsor. That resolves the question the earlier hop left open: the bare name "SF Forward" (and "SF Chamber of Commerce 21st Century Committee") carries the same FPPC id (891575) as "San Francisco Forward sponsored by San Francisco Chamber of Commerce", so it is the Chamber's committee. The rule used is: the FPPC id first, then the name's words, then the sponsor words in a name; anything else is left unmatched and shown as filed. 17 donor families were defined, taking 135 committee giver rows (19 by id, 116 by name). Where a committee's sponsor is "Labor Organizations", no single organisation is named and none is assigned.
3. **Which of those donors publish a priority at a citable link?** Of the 17 families, 11 have a statement in their own words, each quoted from the donor's own page, verified on the page on 2026-10-30 and carried with link and retrieval date in `data/priorities.json` (and, for SEIU Local 2015, `data/priorities_reported.json`): the Chamber, Airbnb, the San Francisco Police Officers Association (POA), SEIU Local 1021, Govern For California, the San Francisco Apartment Association, BOMA San Francisco, GrowSF, Neighbors for a Better San Francisco (Blueprint: its three issue pages and its voter guide positions on Props D, E, F, H, I and J), the San Francisco Association of Realtors (its About page, which states what the association is committed to and that it advocates for legislation supporting its members; a statement of purpose, not a position on a city measure) and SEIU Local 2015 (its own ballot recommendations page, a table, not prose: No on Props D, E and F, Yes on Props H and I; the page's footer disclaimer names "Dignity CA Service Employees International Union SEIU Local 2015", which ties the committee name on the gifts to the local). The other six have no statement in their own words, but each has at least one report by others, kept apart in `data/priorities_reported.json` and section 4a of `funding-map.md`. The maintainer's rule for these: "these don't need to be explicit position statements; fine to read reports from credible news organizations that suggest priorities for large donors or PACs as long as we track sources". Each entry carries its outlet, link, publication and retrieval dates, who the words belong to, whether the page was read in full or only a search-result extract of it, and what it does not show.
   - **Labor Council**: the city's list of qualified measures names it the author of the official opposing argument on Prop D (<https://www.sf.gov/qualified-ballot-measures>); Mission Local reports it gave $20,000 against Prop D and that its Committee on Political Education voted to oppose it; the Chronicle's endorsement editorial quotes its executive director, Kim Tavaglione, on the quadrupling of the signature requirement. Its own endorsements page returned no text to a plain request or to a page fetch.
   - **IFPTE Local 21**: Mission Local (10 April 2026) quotes its president, Bianca Polovina, on city services and jobs, in opposition to the Mayor's layoffs; the Chronicle reports its objection to the Mayor's permitting plan. Neither is about a ballot measure.
   - **Laborers Local 261**: Mission Local (3 August 2026) reports it recommended removing "affordable housing" from the Prop I digest (a position on wording, not on Prop I); the Mayor's office release on the BUILD Act names it in the coalition behind it; the Examiner reports the Mayor's claim that it supports Props D, E and F.
   - **Operating Engineers Local 3**: only the Examiner's report of the Mayor's claim that it supports Props D, E and F. No statement by the union was found.
   - **Emerson Collective**: Mission Local (6 July 2026) reports long support for the Mayor and a reported meeting with him about a San Francisco "branding campaign"; Emerson is a donor to Mission Local, which the article discloses. No priority in Emerson's words, and none stated for the measures it funded.
   - **TWU Local 250-A**: the League of Women Voters' pros and cons guide lists it among a measure's supporters; the extract read does not show which measure.
   The Apartment Association also has reports by others on measures its own pages do not address: Mission Local and The Real Deal (October 2026) report it gave $211,000 to the committee opposing Prop I, which also backs Prop C. The Realtors have no position on a measure in text: the Realtors' November 2026 voter guide (<https://sfrealtors.com/2026/10/29/november-2026-general-election-voter-guide/>) is a single image, the Apartment Association's 2026 slate card PDF holds no extractable text, and its active-legislation pages give status and sponsors only. The strength of the sources differs and the entries say so: a donor quoted by a news outlet (IFPTE 21, the Labor Council's director) is stronger than a reporter's statement of a position or of spending, which is stronger than another party's claim of support (the Mayor's, for the Operating Engineers and Laborers) or a supporter list. Spending on a measure is a record of money, not a priority in the donor's words. `collect_pages.py` re-fetches each reported page and records in `data/reported_check.json` whether the quote is on it. Individuals: of the large individual givers to the Mayor's measure committees, one (Jeremy Stoppelman) has a first-party statement in the earlier survey's class A; Moritz and Chakrabarti are class B, most others C or D.
4. **Do the city's limits mean organisations' money reaches officials mostly as outside spending or through ballot measure committees?** By dollars of gifts, **yes for ballot-measure committees, and for committees for other offices; no single answer for outside spending, which is a separate bucket.** The rules: $500 per source per election to a candidate committee, no corporate, LLC or LLP gifts to candidate committees, no limit on non-candidate committees, candidate-controlled measure committees or independent spending; county central committee candidate committees are also outside the limit (Ethics Commission sources in `funding-map.md` section 6). In the filings, organisations and committees gave $207K of $15.3M (1.4%) to the officials' own supervisor and mayor committees, against $5.59M of $18.6M (30.0%) to the ballot-measure committees carrying the Mayor's name and $220K of $983K (22.4%) to their committees for another office. Of the $6.33M that organisations gave across all committee classes, 88% went to the Mayor's measure committees. Outside spending naming the twelve officials (form 496) is $11.06M over all spenders, of which $1.14M is by committees a donor family's names take; it is not added to any gift.

**How each came by.** Each began with a family row in the snapshot (a donor family's gift to a committee tied to the official), then a search of the caption transcripts for the official's words on a subject the donor's own page speaks to. Pairings 1 and 2 use the Mayor's opening remarks at his appearance before the Board (the transcript names him before and after: speaker credit low doubt). Pairing 3 uses a turn opened by "SUPERVISOR DORSEY" (low doubt). Pairings 4 and 5 reuse the quote boundaries and speaker reading of the earlier worked pairings (3 and 5 there) and re-read them from the transcript (the build refuses a quote not word for word); pairing 4's credit is high doubt (the caption renders the name "BALA MAMU" and credit rests on two pages naming Mahmood as sponsor), pairing 5's low.

**How well each is matched.** The strength of the overlap differs and each pairing says so (section N.4): pairing 4 is the same measure (Prop J) with opposite sides on the text; pairing 5 is the same three measures; pairings 1, 2 and 3 are the same subject only (Muni; the city budget and general fund; the inclusionary fee vote and the Prop C package) and say so. A reporter should read 1 to 3 as leads on a subject, not as statements about each other. The money is of three kinds a reader must not confuse: a ballot-measure committee carrying the Mayor's name (1, 2), the official's own committees for supervisor or for another office (3, 4) and old committees for another office (5, years before the supervisor seat). Pairing 5's gifts date from 2012 and 2015.

**What the money search found beyond the pairings.** Money from donor families with a published priority reached: the Mayor's measure committees (Chamber $499K, Airbnb's sponsored committee $500K, POA Issues PAC $10K; Emerson Collective $700K and TWU Local 250-A $50K have no statement found); Dorsey (Realtors $20K, Apartment Association $11K and POA $8K, all into his officeholder committees); Mahmood ($18,000), Sauter ($4,500) and Sherrill ($2,500) from committees named for Govern For California, in gifts of $500 to $5,000; Wong (BOMA's committee $9,999 into a committee formed to support him, a third-party committee) and Chan (BOMA's committee $20,000 into a committee formed to oppose her).

## Keeping the buckets apart

- **Gifts, per committee.** Schedule A rows of the latest version of each filing (the dataset keeps no earlier versions). Totals are per committee; no total joins committees, because they are different recipients. Unitemized receipts carry no name and are not attributed.
- **24-hour reports (form 497 part 1)** are listed apart. A 24-hour row with the same committee, date and amount as a Schedule A row is a repeat and is listed, not counted; one without is "no Schedule A row yet" with its own total. In the Mayor's two measure committees 24-hour-only rows total $1.02M (D, E & F) and $131K (H), outside the Schedule A figures above.
- **Outside spending (form 496)** is by the spending committee. It is not a gift to any committee tied to the official, and it is never added to one. A committee that is tied to the official and also spends (Believe in SF, Lurie for Mayor 2024: $6.73M received in Schedule A and $3.74M of form 496 spending supporting the Mayor in 2024) appears in section 1 as a committee and in section 5 as a spender.
- **Self-funding** is inside the individuals' column: the Mayor's own name accounts for $10.74M of the individual money in his own committee. Section 6 of `funding-map.md` lists it.

## Where the sources fall short

- **Control of measure committees is inferred from the name, not read from a filing.** No Form 410 (statement of organization) was read for any committee, so no filing here says that the Mayor or a supervisor controls a named committee. What supports the tie: (1) the state's committee-name rule, 2 CCR section 18402(c)(1), requires the name of a committee controlled by a candidate to include the last name of each controlling candidate (<https://www.fppc.ca.gov/content/dam/fppc/NS-Documents/LegalDiv/Regulations/Index/Chapter4/18402.pdf>), so a candidate-controlled committee carries the surname and a search of committee names by surname finds it (the rule is written for candidates; officeholder committees are named under Regulation 18531.62(c)(2), which the same section cites, my reading that the two together cover a sitting official); (2) the Commission's election dashboards list the Mayor's two measure committees, ids 1489257 and 1479782, under names reading "Mayor Lurie's Ballot Measure Committee" for the 2 June 2026 election (<https://campaign.sfethics.org/elections/2026-06-02/committees/CA1433436>), today under the names "Mayor Lurie's Yes on D, E & F Committee" and "Mayor Lurie's Yes on H Committee" (<https://campaign.sfethics.org/elections/2026-11-03/committees/CA1489257.html>, <https://campaign.sfethics.org/elections/2026-11-03/committees/CA1479782.html>), as primarily formed measure committees; the dashboard's own definition of a candidate-controlled committee is a generic one; (3) Mission Local calls them "the mayor's committees" and says the Muni committee is "sponsored by Mayor Lurie" (<https://missionlocal.org/2026/08/sf-election-campaign-finance-november/>). No supervisor has a ballot-measure committee carrying their surname in either dataset; a measure committee a supervisor controls would, under the name rule, carry the surname and would have been found. Whether "Believe in SF, Lurie for Mayor 2024" and the committees named for supervisors are controlled by them is likewise not stated in a filing read here; the committees formed to support or oppose an official are primarily formed committees whose control the data does not say.
- **Govern For California's chapter committees are tied by its own pages, press and third-party records, not by a registration.** Its "Who We Are" page lists its independent political action committee chapters (including San Francisco, SF Public Schools, East Bay and Southern California) and its footer names Govern For California, a registered 501(c)(4) (<https://www.governforcalifornia.org/who-we-are>); Mission Local reported in 2024 that it gave $500 each to supervisor candidates through eight chapters (<https://missionlocal.org/2024/09/s-f-ethics-complaint-targets-govern-for-california-group-that-bundles-political-donations/>); Transparency USA's record of the state Govern for California Network Committee lists payments to nine chapter committees (<https://www.transparencyusa.org/ca/committee/govern-for-california-network-committee-1383984-rcp>); CalMatters' Digital Democracy lists the chapter committee names under the organisation (<https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/organizations/-15028>). The chapter committees are state committees: the Commission lists them as state-level committees with Cal-Access ids that match the ids on the filed rows (for example 1416440, the San Francisco chapter), but their Cal-Access registrations, which name the sponsor, were not read. Unlike the Chamber's committee, which was matched by an id that resolves in the Filers dataset, these are matched by name; the family note in `data/families.json` carries the same sources.
- **Committees for other offices** (Democratic County Central Committee, College Board) are the official's own, and the Commission's report says county central committee candidate committees are outside the $500 limit; a gift of $2,500 from the POA to Dorsey's officeholder committee for that office is therefore not a breach of the supervisor limit and is shown with its committee name so a reader sees which office it was for.
- **Dates.** Several supervisors' committees are terminated, so recent giving to them is not in the data; Mandelman and Walton show no 2026 committee in either dataset.
- **Names.** Family membership is by FPPC id, then name; a person must read the names as filed. Names that look related but carry no id and no sponsor words (for example a union's "COPE" fund under another name) would be missed.

## How this serves the objectives

- *Everything shown is current and correct*: quotes are re-read from the transcripts word for word at build; statements are re-read from the donors' pages at collection and the run stops if one is gone; reported priorities are re-checked against their pages and marked when the quote is not found; money is stamped with the dataset's load date; each pairing says which kind of committee received the money.
- *Let a reporter quickly understand pay-to-play speech*: section 4 of the map gives a reporter a start from the money (the families that gave, the officials that received, and whether the family has a citable statement), and each pairing is a one-screen lead. Nobody was timed; the key result that waits on timing reporters is not met here.

## How to regenerate

`python collect.py` (network: DataSF queries, about two minutes, and the donors' pages) then `python build_map.py` (no network; it also rewrites the cards block of this file between its markers). `python collect.py --money` and `--pages` run either half (`--pages` also re-checks the reported priorities against their pages). `python -m unittest discover -s tests` checks that quotes are verbatim, that every pairing is complete, that 24-hour repeats are not counted and that no text uses a causal word. Standard library only.
