# Comparable evidence on how reporter-facing and civic data tools show value

**Kind of document: a survey of published sources.** Pages marked "(extract)" were read only as search extracts, not in full, and carry the marker (unverified) for anything specific taken from them. Nothing here is from the site's own users.

## How newsrooms and civic-tech groups show value

- **Impact categories (read in full).** The Reynolds Journalism Institute (2 February 2026) reviewed the impact tracking of ProPublica, CIR/Reveal, The Markup, The Marshall Project and Documented. The categories it found common are legislative change, improved media coverage, community benefit, individual benefit, institutional change, improved transparency and advocacy. For "improved media coverage" the example is more than twenty outlets running their own stories after one election survey. Source: <https://rjionline.org/news/when-its-time-to-brag-about-your-work-how-to-define-and-measure-impact/>. For this site only improved media coverage and, loosely, improved transparency are within reach early.
- **Asking people is part of the method (read in full).** The same page advises putting an email on the story, offering office hours and short surveys, and treating impact as something you report out by asking people who would know. It also says some changes are slow.
- **Catalogues of events, not counts of views (extracts).** The Markup publishes a template for logging "impact events" tied to the story; ProPublica records outcomes only when a clear link is reasonable and keeps noting them for years; a local newspaper began its tracker as a simple form used by a seven-person team before building a database. Sources: <https://themarkup.org/levelup/2022/12/01/how-the-markup-tracks-impact-and-how-your-newsroom-can-too>, <https://www.cjr.org/tow_center_reports/newslynx_a_tool_for_newsroom_impact_measurement.php>, <https://www.cjr.org/united_states_project/impact-tracker-journal-news-lohud.php> (unverified). The shape fits one person: a short log with a date, who, what and a link.
- **Counting beyond clicks (extract).** Knight Foundation and Network Impact's guide to assessing civic tech says measuring means more than counting clicks, views and downloads. Source: <https://bethkanter.org/methods-metrics/> (unverified).

## What a reporter-facing data tool has reported as value

- **Digital Democracy (CalMatters, read in full).** Reported early signs after its April 2024 launch: stories it made possible (a list of about nineteen), two stories that prompted legislators to call for change, more than half of state lawmakers featured in CalMatters' reporting, and lawmakers' own remarks. The text says it "doesn't do journalism" but is a tool for journalists. It is run by a newsroom with a dedicated reporter, so its figures are not a baseline for one person. Source: <https://calmatters.org/inside-the-newsroom/2024/07/digital-democracy-impacts/>.
- **Alerts for reporters (extracts).** A trade-press piece and a conference transcript describe a "tip sheet" system that lets a reporter keep tabs on an individual lawmaker, for journalists only; the speaker at the conference says the tool lets a busy local reporter set it up. Sources: <https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/digital-democracy,251496>, <https://voiceofsandiego.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/VOSD-Politifest-2024-_-Introduction-to-Digital-Democracy-CalMatters-1.pdf> (unverified). This is the nearest published sign of what reporters might want next, and no use figures for it were read.

## Who the local reporters are

- The San Francisco Ethics Commission says its dashboards are made for voters, journalists, staff and regulated communities "to find, interpret, and cite", and that reporters from Mission Local, the San Francisco Standard and the San Francisco Chronicle use them. Its fiscal 2023-24 report says more than fifteen local journalists attended a campaign finance workshop in February 2024 and calls local journalists some of the primary users of the data. Sources: <https://sfethics.org/ethics/2026/07/july-10-2026-agenda-item-8-electronic-disclosure-and-data-analysis-edda-division-report.html>, <https://sfethics.org/ethics/2024/10/ethics-commission-annual-report-for-fiscal-year-2023-2024.html> (unverified, extracts). The Commission's own tools are also the closest substitute a San Francisco reporter has for this site's money half.

## What surveys of civic-tech users show about reading satisfaction

- **mySociety, "Who Benefits From Civic Technology?" (read in full).** 3,705 survey responses from users of sites including TheyWorkForYou and GovTrack. The majority achieved what they set out to do, over 95% on each participating platform said they would use the site again, and the report warns of the human tendency to give positive answers and of self-selection. A floor of 100 responses was too high for most sites to reach in four to five months. It also records that these sites' users were disproportionately older and educated, and that sites run by small teams have outages that stop a survey running. Source: <https://research.mysociety.org/html/who-benefits/>. Lessons for this site: an intent question ("would you use it again") barely discriminates; counts of reporters will be small; the survey must not depend on the site being up.

## What was not found

- No account from a reporter of what they miss in a lookup tool that pairs speech and funding.
- No figure for how many returns, citations or corrections a one-person local site should expect in its first weeks.
- Nothing on corrections as a sign of value: none of the impact schemes read treats corrections received as a value measure. Corrections are covered here as an engagement sign by inference (my reading).
