# Early reader value reading: which signs would show reporters find the site worth a visit, and what the first readers say

**Kind of hop: a survey.** It surveys how comparable reporter-facing and civic-data projects show that reporters value them, checks each sign against what one person running the site alone can count (and against what the first version of the site records today), and sets up the first reading. It makes no decisions and writes no measure or threshold.

**The short answer: the signs are weighed, but the first reading itself does not exist, and none can be taken from early users yet.** The maintainer says: "No reporter has used it and I have none to ask." No usage record, no reporter's words and no count of any sign exists for the first version of the site. Questions 2 and 3 are therefore answered only from comparable projects, which is not the same as the site's own first readers. What the files give is the weighing (question 1), a ready sheet for taking the reading (`first-reading-sheet.md`) and the list of what must be added to the site before a sign can be counted at all.

## What was found, strongest first

1. **"Another outlet used what the site found" is the sign the comparable newsrooms agree on.** A review of the impact tracking of five nonprofit newsrooms (ProPublica, CIR/Reveal, The Markup, The Marshall Project, Documented) lists "improved media coverage" among the categories they have in common: another outlet replicating or localising the work. The one reporter-facing tool surveyed that reports value (CalMatters' Digital Democracy) reports it as a list of stories the tool made possible. For this site that sign is "stories citing the site". One person can count it, but only by hand: searching the web and news for the site's name and card links each week, plus reporters saying so. It undercounts (print, broadcast, screenshots, no link) and, in the first weeks, is likely to be zero or a handful. See `signs-and-countability.md`.
2. **Return visits by reporters cannot be counted today, and cannot be told apart from other readers.** The site has no accounts and its request log records only method, path, status and timing: no visitor identifier, referrer or user agent. A count of returning visitors needs something added, and even then it counts readers, not reporters, unless reporters arrive by a link given to them. This is the sign a lone operator is least able to read as "reporters".
3. **Corrections sent in are countable by one person, but the site has no way to send one yet.** There is no contact route on the cards or in the footer. The maintainer wants one: "I'd like the reporters to be able to send feedback to the admins, and that feedback should be in an admin queue. But they shouldn't directly send email." A person reading an admin queue can count and sort entries by kind, card and week, provided each entry records those. They mean two things at once (a reporter engaged enough to check, and a possible error), so they need sorting by kind before they can show value. (My reading.)
4. **With no reporter to ask, the readings come from reporters who turn up unasked.** The cheapest direct reading would have been asking named reporters, but none is known. What remains is the feedback queue (signs 2, 5, 6, and 11 for those who say who they are), a weekly web and news search for the site (sign 1), and requests per card path from the log (sign 7). Self-reported "would use again" answers are weak evidence: in mySociety's survey of 3,705 users of civic-tech sites, over 95% on each participating platform said they would use the site again, and the report itself warns about positive answers and self-selection. A question about what they did (used a card in a story, checked a source, wrote back) is stronger than one about what they intend.
5. **The pool of reporters is small and findable.** The Ethics Commission reports that its February 2024 campaign finance workshop drew more than fifteen local journalists, and that reporters from Mission Local, the San Francisco Standard and the San Francisco Chronicle use its dashboards (extracts only). So thresholds are in units of single reporters and outlets, not hundreds. (My reading.)
6. **Small civic sites struggle to gather even 100 survey answers.** mySociety set a floor of 100 responses and most sites could not reach it in four to five months. A first reading from a one-person site will be interviews and counts of tens, not statistics.

## What the maintainer's answers settle, and what stays open

- Settled: no early reporter readers exist; feedback goes to an admin queue and not to a published or emailed address.
- Open: whether the referring page of card requests, and a daily visit count, may be recorded. The maintainer did not answer. Signs 1, 4, 7, 8 and 9 hang on it; 2, 5 and 6 do not.

## By charter question

1. **Signs and what one person can count: answered** (`signs-and-countability.md`).
2. **What the first weeks show for each sign: not found.** The site's first version has no usage record, no reporter has used it, and nothing was found of its first weeks. Published comparable figures are not a baseline for a one-city site with no newsroom behind it.
3. **What early reporter users say would bring them back, and what they miss: not found for the site's own readers; partly from comparable tools.** There are no early reporter users, so no words from them exist. From comparable tools: per-lawmaker alerts for reporters ("tip sheets" at Digital Democracy), full searchable transcripts, and material built to be found, interpreted and cited (`comparable-evidence.md`). Whether alerts are what this site's reporters ask for is unknown.

## Files

- `signs-and-countability.md`: twelve candidate signs, what each shows, who can count it, how, what it misses, and what the first version records today.
- `comparable-evidence.md`: what the sources say, with links; what bears on this site and what does not.
- `first-reading-sheet.md`: counting rules, a weekly sheet and the questions to put to any reporter who gets in touch, with blank tables to fill.
- `open-questions.md`: what was not found, what needs the maintainer, and what is unverified.
