# Pairing name options: what a card should be called so a first-time reader understands it

**Kind of hop: a survey** of how comparable money-in-politics sites and newsrooms word the same connection, and of the words this site already uses, written as findings. It makes no decision. No reader was asked; what each word suggests is my reading, and `reader-check.md` is a sheet for asking a few people.

## What was found

1. **A card has six places that need words, not one** (`name-options.md`). Only one is the name of the unit (pairing, card, pair). The others are the money heading, the donor's side, the official's side, the strip's guard sentence and the giver's name. They can be chosen separately.
2. **No comparable site names the unit.** Digital Democracy keeps Financials, Bill Alignment and Testimony as separate sections; Voter's Edge kept priorities, endorsements and top donors apart and called its published unit a "card"; the SF Ethics dashboards and the newsrooms describe donors in the writer's voice (`comparable-sites.md`). The neutral words in use are the filing's: contributions, contributors, donors. The colourful ones sit in headlines: "bankrolling", "backed by", "poured", and Digital Democracy's own "influence" for gifts and travel. So the card needs its own word with a short explanation, or descriptive words and no unit noun.
3. **Words that fail the Vision's non-goals** (`name-options.md`, "Words that fail the tests"): for causation, influence, pay-to-play, buy, bankroll, payment, ask, echo, money trail, and, more mildly, backed by, funding, support, linked to, wants; for partisan sound, special interests, big money, dark money, elite, agenda, allies and the camp labels (moderate, progressive, pro-housing, tech-backed). The test for a partisan word is whether the donor would use it of itself.
4. **Words that misdescribe the card**: match, alignment and echo imply agreement, but the current cards include different positions and same-subject-only readings; "gave to the Mayor" misplaces money that went to committees; any "then", "after" or arrow implies a time order the card does not show (the money in Card 1 runs past the date of the words). The current heading "Money in, then the donor's priority" contains one.
5. **"Pairing", the word now used,** passes the causation and partisan tests and carries only a mild agreement hint. It has no prior meaning for a first-time reader here, which the lede's explanation covers.
6. **The money next to the words invites the reading by itself.** One law-school experiment (snippet only) and the comments under a neutral data article both show readers supplying a cause and a side (`comparable-sites.md`, section 4). A name can avoid hints; it cannot remove that, so the explanation and the order carry weight.
7. **Where the name appears** (`where-the-name-appears.md`): three words (pairing, card, pair) are used for one object at three stages; five public places should share one word (title, lede, count and empty-state, strip, footer); the maintainer's review screens may keep a stage word; the public path `/pairings/` would need a redirect if the word changes.
8. **Spending that does not go to the official's committees has three names in San Francisco sources** (`comparable-sites.md`, section 8): the filing's "independent expenditure" (Form 496), the Ethics Commission's "third-party spending", and Mission Local's "outside spending", which it uses beside "independent committees". None of the pages read calls one the usual term.
9. **A card line that names the idea it denies carries a risk, by analogy.** Research on corrections finds denials that repeat a claim can raise its familiarity, and advises emphasising the facts (`comparable-sites.md`, section 9). It concerns retracted claims, not disclaimers on a card; no study of the card's situation was found.
10. **The Objective "pay-to-play speech"** uses a phrase flagged in finding 3. The Objective stands; the site's visible words should not carry it (`open-questions.md`).

## What was not found

No reader evidence on any candidate word; no shared convention for the unit; no page on opensecrets.org was read (its scope comes from Ballotpedia, its use of "outside spending" from a chart crediting it); MapLight's current site is a software vendor's and Voter's Edge has ended; no style-guide rule on wording a contribution beside a quote (`open-questions.md`).

## Files

- `name-options.md`: candidates per slot, what each suggests, flags, and the words that fail the tests.
- `comparable-sites.md`: how Digital Democracy, the SF Ethics Commission, San Francisco newsrooms, MapLight, Voter's Edge and ProPublica word the connection, what words San Francisco sources use for Form 496 spending, research on denials, and how readers took one article.
- `where-the-name-appears.md`: every place the word appears and whether each needs the same one.
- `reader-check.md`: a short sheet for asking three to six people, with the maintainer choosing whom.
- `open-questions.md`: what was not found, what to ask, and the claims not verified.
