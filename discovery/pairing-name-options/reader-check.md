# Reader check: a short sheet for asking a few people what each candidate word means to them

**Kind of hop: a survey instrument**, not a result. No reader has been asked; nothing here is evidence about readers until it is used. It leaves the choice of words to the maintainer. Candidates are those of `name-options.md`.

## Who and how many

Three to six people, each alone, about ten minutes. At least one who has never seen the site and at least one reporter or watchdog (Objectives 1 and 2). Do not ask anyone to say which name they "like"; ask what they take it to mean. The maintainer names the people.

## Materials

One real card from `../official-funding-map/pairing-cards.html` (for example Card 4, where the two take different positions, and Card 1, where they share a subject only), with the unit's name, the zone headings and the strip covered. Show one card per person per round, and rotate which candidate set goes with which card.

## Part 1: what is this? (before any word is shown)

1. "In your own words, what is this?"
2. "What would you call it to a friend?" (Record what they say; these are free candidates.)
3. "Who gave what to whom, as far as you can tell?"

## Part 2: one candidate at a time

Show the card with one candidate in the unit slot. Use the pairs below, one per person, never all.

| Set | Unit | Money heading | Donor side | Words side | Strip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Pairing | Money in | Stated priority | The official's exact words | Reading, not a finding |
| B | Side by side | Contributions | Stated position | Said at the meeting | What the two share, and where they differ |
| C | Words and money | Money given to committees tied to the official | In its own words | On the record | Our comparison, not a conclusion |
| D | Overlap | Donations | Public statement | Words credited to the official | How to read this card |

The strip's closing sentence is the other thing to vary between people: one hears "No cause is claimed", another "What was said, what was given and when, nothing about why" (`name-options.md`, slot 5). Research on denials suggests the first can raise the idea it names (`comparable-sites.md`, section 9); question 5 below is how to see whether it does here.

Ask, after each:

4. "What does the word on top tell you this is?"
5. Scale 1 to 5, "This card says the donor's money led the official to say these words." (1 = disagree, 5 = agree.) Then: "What in the card made you answer that?"
6. "Does the card seem to be for or against anyone? Who?" (A word that draws a side is flagged P.)
7. "Do the donor and the official seem to agree, disagree, or neither?" (A word that draws "agree" on a disagreeing card is flagged A.)
8. "Who received the money?" (A word that draws "the official" is flagged R.)
9. "Did the order of the three parts say anything about what happened first?" (Flag T.)

## Part 3: a ranked question (optional)

Give the person four index cards with four unit words, no explanation. Ask which they would expect to find if they wanted: (a) a list of what officials said, (b) a list of who funds officials, (c) a list of things that might be worth checking. Record the card chosen for each.

## Recording

One row per person per set: answers to 1 to 9, the scale value in 5, the free words from 2, and any word the person used that is on the flagged list in `name-options.md` (for example "bought", "influence", "paid", "corrupt"). Report counts per set, not percentages, and never present three to six people as a sample of the public.

## What would count as a problem

- Any person scoring 4 or 5 on question 5 for a set. The wording or the card, not the person, is what to examine.
- Two or more people drawing a side (question 6) from the same word.
- A reporter saying the unit word is unclear but a plain description is not: a sign that the lede needs the description more than the card needs the noun.
