# Name options: candidate words for each thing a card shows, what each suggests, and which fail the Vision's non-goals

**Kind of hop: a survey** (of how outside sites and newsrooms word the same connection, and of the words this site already uses), written as findings. It makes no decision; the "flags" below say which words fail a test the Vision sets, and the choice among the rest stays with the maintainer. Summary and links: `findings.md`.

**How far the "suggests" columns can be trusted.** No reader was asked. What each word suggests to someone who has never seen the site is my reading of the word and of what the cards contain; the outside sites' own usage is sourced in `comparable-sites.md`. `reader-check.md` is a short sheet for finding out from a few people.

## What a card shows, so the names can be checked against it

From Project Memory and `../official-funding-map/pairing-cards.md`: a card puts three things side by side and each has its own zone.

1. **Money** that givers named like the donor gave to committees tied to the official (own, officeholder, or ballot-measure committees carrying the official's name), one line per committee, never summed across committees, with outside spending kept apart.
2. **The donor's priority**: the donor's own words with organisation, page date and link (Project Memory also allows reports from credible news organisations, with sources tracked).
3. **The official's exact words** from a city meeting transcript, with a speaker-credit doubt badge.

Plus a strip that says what the words and the priority have in common and where they differ.

Five facts about the cards decide which words are accurate, and each rules out a family of words:

| Fact | Where it comes from | Words it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| The money goes to **committees tied to the official**, not to the official | `pairing-cards.md`, Card 1 (a ballot-measure committee carrying the Mayor's name) and Card 3 (officeholder committee for another office) | "gave to the Mayor", "the official's money", "paid" |
| The words and the priority are **sometimes on different sides** of the same measure, and in three of five cards only on the same subject | `pairing-cards.md`, Cards 1 to 4 ("Where they differ") | match, alignment, echo, support, "lines up" |
| The money can be **dated after the words** or years before them (Card 1: money to 23 September 2026, words 14 July 2026; Card 5: gifts from 2012 and 2015) | `pairing-cards.md`, Cards 1 and 5 | then, after, before, following, "in return", an arrow |
| The **speaker credit is in doubt** on some cards (the badge is low, medium or high) | `pairing-cards.md` | "said by" as a label where the badge is not low |
| The donor's words are **not always the donor's page** once Project Memory's news reports are used, and not always a position | Project Memory | "in the donor's own words" as a fixed label |

## The six slots

A card has more than one place that needs a word. They do not all need the same word (see `where-the-name-appears.md`). The first slot is the one the charter question asks about, the other five are the zone and strip headings.

Flags used in the tables: **C** hints that the money caused the words (or the reverse), **P** can sound partisan, **A** implies agreement between the words and the priority, **R** misplaces who received the money, **T** implies a time order the card does not show, **J** is jargon a first-time reader will not know.

### Slot 1. The unit: what to call one card's worth of words, priority and money

| Candidate | What it suggests to a first-time reader (my reading) | Flags | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pairing** (now used) | Two things matched; ambiguous which two. Also suggests a deliberate matchmaking, and the card holds three things. Needs a line of explanation, which the lede already gives. | A (mild) | Used for the page URLs, the database, the code and every earlier findings document; a first-time reader has no prior meaning for it here. |
| **Side by side** (as in "a side-by-side") | Two or three things put next to each other with nothing claimed. | none | Awkward as a noun in a list heading ("12 side-by-sides"); fine as a verb phrase in a lede. |
| **Comparison** | The site compared the two and found something. Suggests analysis and, to some, a verdict on which is "right". | A (mild) | Fits the "reading" strip. Reads as the site's judgement more than a display. |
| **Overlap** | The words and the priority share a subject. | A (mild) | Accurate for cards that share a measure or subject, inaccurate for a card whose reading says "not comparable on the text" (Card 3). |
| **Match** | The words and the priority agree, and an algorithm found it. | A (strong) | Fails on cards whose reading says the two take different positions (Card 4). A person reviews each card, so "match" also misstates how it was found. |
| **Alignment** | The official and the donor are on the same side, and possibly allied. | A, P | Digital Democracy uses "Bill Alignment" for a person's recorded position on a bill (`comparable-sites.md`), which is the same word in a different job. |
| **Connection / link / tie** | There is a relationship beyond what the filings show; "linked to" is how a headline insinuates (recalled). | C (strong) | Fails the non-goal: the card shows two facts that sit side by side, not a relationship between them. |
| **Echo / follows / mirrors** | One repeats the other, so one came first. | C, T | Gives the donor's words priority over the official's. Ruled out. |
| **Record / entry** | A neutral row in a database. | none | Says nothing about what is in it; "record" can read as an official record (overstated authority). |
| **File / case / case file** | An investigation of someone. | C | Ruled out. |
| **Lead** | A tip for a reporter, so something may be wrong. Worked-pairing findings use it for a reporter's look. | C (mild) | Useful word for reporters (Objective 1), but it tells a general reader there is a story. |
| **Words and money** (or "Words, priority and money") | Exactly what is on the card, in plain words, in an order that puts the words first. | T (depends on order) | A description more than a name; names the two outer parts and drops the donor's priority unless it is spelled out. |
| **Three-part card / "the card"** | Just a card. | none | Names the form, not the content; what a card is "of" must be said beside it. |
| **Money trail, follow the money, influence, pay-to-play, bought, bankrolled** | The money caused the words. | C (strong) | Ruled out by the first non-goal. See "Words that fail the tests". |

No candidate in the list is free of every flag except the descriptive ones (side by side, record, words and money, the card). The ones that need a sentence of explanation are the ones that are also the least misleading.

### Slot 2. The money part (heading of the left zone, first half)

The zone now reads "Money in, then the donor's priority".

| Candidate | What it suggests (my reading) | Flags | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Money in** (now used) | Money coming in, to whom unclear. | R (mild) | Short and plain. The Mayor-named committees are not the Mayor. |
| **Contributions** | The filing word. Neutral, and the word the Ethics Commission's data uses. Also reads as "contributed to the outcome". | none (small C) | Matches the "Schedule A" line a reporter will check. A resident may not know it is political giving. |
| **Donations** | Plain; slightly charitable. | none | "Donor" and "donation" belong together. |
| **Money given to committees tied to the official** | Exactly what the filings show. | none | Long for a heading; fits a line under it. |
| **Campaign money** | The money was for the campaign, not for the speech. | none | May read as a statement about the official's election rather than about the committees listed. |
| **Funding / who is funding** | The SF Ethics Commission's dashboard uses "who's funding" (`comparable-sites.md`). | C (mild) | "Funding the official" suggests the money keeps the official going. |
| **Gifts** | A legal category in Form 700 disclosures (read in the Digital Democracy methodology), a personal gift to the official. | R, C | Wrong word for committee contributions. |
| **Payments** | A purchase of something. ProPublica's physician pages use it where the payments were for talks and consulting. | C (strong) | Not accurate here: nothing is bought, and payment implies a service. |
| **Backing / support / bankrolling** | The donor is behind the official. | C, P | See "Words that fail the tests". |
| **Outside spending** (a separate line) | Money spent without being given to the official's committees. | none | The usual newsroom and watchdog term for form 496 spending (recalled); the card keeps it apart. |

### Slot 3. The donor's side (heading of the donor's bubble)

The bubble now reads "{Donor}: stated priority".

| Candidate | What it suggests (my reading) | Flags | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stated priority** (now used) | What the donor has said matters most to it. | none (small overclaim) | "Priority" can overclaim: a page recommending a vote on a measure is a position, not necessarily a top priority. "Stated" does the work of saying the donor said it. |
| **Position** / **Stated position** | A side on a measure. | none | Accurate for voter guides, not for the news-report priorities Project Memory allows. |
| **What the donor says it wants** | The donor is asking the official for something. | C (strong) | The word "wants" sets the donor up as a requester. Vision's own sentence uses "what their funders want"; the Vision is a description for the maintainer, not a label. |
| **Ask** | A lobbying request. | C (strong) | Ruled out. |
| **In its own words** | The donor's quote, verbatim. | none | True only while every statement is the donor's own page; false for a news report of a priority. A fixed heading cannot cover both. |
| **Its public statement** | Anything it has said in public. | none | Covers the donor's own page; a news report about a donor is not its statement. |
| **Goals / agenda** | An agenda is often a hidden one. | C, P (mild) | "Agenda" is a word opponents use against each other. |
| **Interest / special interest** | A pressure group. | P (strong) | The cards' own sources use it as an attack ("special-interest led", in the POA page quoted in Card 2). |
| **Platform / endorsement** | A party platform, or a vote for a candidate. | P | An endorsement is a different fact: Card 5's page is about endorsements as well as measures. |

The strip now opens "Reading, not a finding."

| Candidate | What it suggests (my reading) | Flags | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reading, not a finding** (now used) | A person's reading, which has not been confirmed. | J (mild) | "Finding" is a researcher's word; "reading" can be heard as "reading matter". The sentence is the strongest guard in the site's wording. |

### Slot 6. The giver ("donor") and the whole-site tagline

| Candidate | What it suggests (my reading) | Flags | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Donor** (now used) | A person or group that gave money. | none | The word Mission Local's data article uses ("Major donors"). Covers PACs, unions and chapters. |
| **Contributor** | The filing word. | J (mild) | The filings and the SF Ethics site use it. |
| **Funder** | The Vision's word. A group that pays for things. | C (mild) | "Funder" says the money funds the official's work. |
| **Giver** | Plain. | none | The cards already say "giver as filed". |

A tagline for the site must also carry the three-part content. The title tag now says "San Francisco officials, donors' priorities and money", which names the three parts in a neutral order, officials first. That order is a point in its favour: the money is not first.

## Words that fail the tests

### Hints that the money caused the words (fails non-goal 1)

Strong: **influence** (Digital Democracy's own name for gifts, travel and behests; `comparable-sites.md`), **pay-to-play** (a legal term for contributions that disqualify a donor from a contract or office, which is exactly an exchange; see `comparable-sites.md`), **buy / bought / purchase / payoff / return on investment / reward**, **quid pro quo** (the Robertson study's frame for corruption), **bankroll** (Mission Local's and the San Francisco Chronicle's headline verb), **payment** (as the name of a political contribution), **ask** (as the donor's side), **echo / follows** (direction), **money trail / follow the money** (suggests the trail leads somewhere; recalled, from the phrase's origin in a Watergate-era line).

Moderate: **backed by / backer / backing**, **funding / funded by** said of the official, **support** (as the money's name), **linked to / ties / connection**, **response**, **wants**, **lines up with**, **behind**. ("Tied to the official" in the site's own wording describes a committee's registration, not the donor; it is accurate there and should stay on the committee.)

Time-order words the card cannot support: **then, after, before, following, since, in return, in exchange** and an arrow. The left zone's heading "Money in, then the donor's priority" contains "then" and an order (money, then priority) that a reader may take as a sequence.

**Objective wording.** The objective "let a reporter quickly understand pay-to-play speech" uses the phrase flagged above. The Vision's non-goals rule out implying that donations cause what politicians say. The objective stands as written; the site's visible words should not carry it. Whether it was meant as a label or as shorthand is a question for the maintainer (`open-questions.md`).

### Can sound partisan (fails non-goal 2)

**special interests, big money, dark money, billionaire, oligarch, elite, donor class, corporate** (as a label), **grassroots, agenda, allies, machine, establishment, lobby**, and political-camp descriptors used for San Francisco groups such as **moderate, progressive, pro-housing, tech-backed** (the last in Mission Local's own text). These are words one side uses about the other, and the first cards cover a business chamber, a police union, a labor union and an advocacy group of the same kind side by side, so a word sounding hostile to one of them is partisan against it. The test for a word: would the donor use it of itself? "Donor", "contributor" and "giver" pass; "special interest" and "big money" do not (the cards' own sources use "special-interest" as an attack).

### Misdescribe what the card shows (accuracy)

**Match, alignment, echo, "lines up"** (agreement, when four of the five current cards are not agreement), **gave to the Mayor** (money is to committees), **said by** with a medium or high credit doubt.

## Candidates that pass all three tests

Plain, descriptive words pass the causation and partisan tests: **side by side**, **record**, **words and money**, **donor**, **contributor**, **giver**, **contributions**, **donations**, **money given to committees tied to the official**, **exact words**, **stated priority**, **what the two share**. Each is either short and vague (needs explanation) or accurate and long. "Pairing" passes the causation and partisan tests and carries only the mild agreement hint above. It is a word the site invented, so the short explanation in the lede is what carries the meaning; no source read in this run uses it for this connection (see `comparable-sites.md`).

## What this leaves for the card redesign

- Whether to keep **pairing** with its lede, or pick one of the descriptive words and carry **side by side** as the verb in the lede. The choice is the maintainer's; reader-check is a way to inform it.
- Every heading with "then" or an arrow, and every "no cause is claimed" sentence, can be replaced without choosing a name at all.
- Slots 2, 3 and 4 are independent of slot 1: any unit name can keep "stated priority" and "exact words".
- If the donor's side admits news reports, its heading needs a form that works for both ("{Donor}: stated priority" with the source type, or "reported by {outlet}").
