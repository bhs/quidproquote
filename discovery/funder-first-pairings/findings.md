# Funder-first pairings: starting from where a donor's money reached an official, how often is there a remark to show, who said it, and what needed a person

**Kind of hop: a survey** of sources outside the project (the city's caption transcripts and meeting minutes on Legistar, the Ethics Commission's campaign finance data as already collected by the funding map, and the donors' own pages and press reports as already collected there), written as **findings**. It also ships the program that produced the figures, `run.py`, so they can be regenerated. It makes no decisions.

The approach it tests: take the funded pairs of the funding map (`../official-funding-map/`), search each official's turns in the meetings for words on the donor's stated subject, credit each passage to its speaker from the caption text, check the credit against the minutes, and lay out five complete pairings without suggesting the money caused the words.

## What was found, strongest first

- **Five complete pairings can be built from the funded pairs, but only from a small share of them.** Of 60 funded pairs that reached a committee tied to the official (62 in all; 2 reached only a committee formed to support or oppose the official), **8 (13%) have a remark a reviewer judged to be on the donor's stated subject** in the 11 sampled meetings, 5 more (8%) only the same broad subject, and **47 (78%) have none**. All 62 pairs, with the money, the outcome and the reason, are in `results.md`. The five pairings are in `pairings.md`: Mahmood and Govern For California, Wong and the Police Officers Association, Walton and SEIU 1021, Melgar and the Apartment Association, Fielder and IFPTE Local 21. Each has the exact words with meeting and stamp, the credit with its doubt, the donor's words with link and date, the money by committee and filing, and a reading that claims no cause. The gifts in them are small ($500 to $10,500 per committee, all to the official's own committees). The large gifts, to the Mayor's measure committees ($499,000 from the Chamber's committee, $500,000 from Airbnb's), have no fitting remark: the Chamber's has one broad-subject remark (Muni ridership) and Airbnb's none.
- **Donors' own words and press reports fit at similar, low rates.** Searching on the donor's own words, 3 of 19 pairs (16%) have a fitting remark and 3 more a broad one; searching on priorities reported by others, 5 of 48 pair-and-report combinations (10%) fit and 2 are broad (two of those five rest on the Mayor's claim that the Laborers and Operating Engineers back Props D, E and F, not on the unions' words). The difference is small and rests on 3 and 5 pairs, so these data do not show that own words fit more often.
- **A term in common is often chance, and the specific terms are the ones that stand out.** The terms hit 45 passage-and-donor-subject combinations (34 distinct passages): 27 on a specific term (a measure's name or a phrase particular to it), of which 16 fit, 3 were broad and 8 were chance (for example 'Proposition E' in a remark about the 2024 Prop E); 18 on a broad topic term only, of which none fit, 7 were broad and 11 were chance (for example 'tenants' in a drug-free housing ordinance, 'first responders' in a fireworks ordinance). This is the first measure of how many pairs look alike by chance; it rests on one reader and a term list written from the donors' words.
- **The rule that credits speakers is cautious and is wrong at times.** From the caption text alone (a cue naming the member before they speak, closed by the next cue), the rule credits 23.6% of all caption words in the 11 meetings to a named official (51% of the 14 July Board meeting; 8% to 13% of each committee meeting). It cannot credit the Board President's floor remarks at all (Mandelman: no turn), and credits only the Mayor's opening remarks, not his answers. Of the 34 distinct candidate passages it credited, the reviewer agreed with 29, disagreed with 2 (both medium doubt: a passage after four `>>` marks that belonged to Walton, not Melgar, and one that belonged to a member speaking before Melgar's turn) and could not tell on 3. None of its 14 low-doubt credits was contradicted. Searched the other way, the earlier hop's five hand-built pairings: the rule found 3 again as candidates, credited a 4th without a term hit (the Mayor's budget remarks: the donor's subject terms are about Prop I and the general fund), and missed the 5th (Mahmood on Prop J: the captions give his name as 'BALA MAMU' with no title, so no cue credits it).
- **The minutes confirm little, and contradicted nothing.** Minutes text was read for one of the 11 meetings (the 14 July Board meeting, from excerpts typed from the draft minutes; see "How well sourced"). Of the 24 distinct passages in that meeting, the minutes **confirmed 4** (a vote on the side the words state: Dorsey's 'voting no' is on the Noes of Fielder's amendment; a motion Dorsey moved to add 'evictions'; the Mayor addressing the Board, for two passages), were **consistent with 4** (the credited member is a sponsor of the item the words are about: Wong, Melgar), were **silent on 16** and conflicted with none. Floor remarks leave no trace in action minutes, so silence is the usual result and says nothing against the credit.
- **Which steps needed judgement** (Q3): reading each donor's words into search terms (19 subjects), deciding whether a passage is on the subject (45), agreeing or disagreeing with the rule's credit (45), cutting the quote at whole sentences (20), and writing what the two have in common and where they differ (16). The matching of givers to donors (17 families) was a person's step in the funding map and is applied here by rule. **None of these was timed**, and all were done by the agent that wrote this hop, not a reporter; the machine steps take under a second each.

**Not found:** the number of pairings a week the site could expect (the meetings read are 11, from January to July; four of the five pairings come from one Board meeting, 14 July, which voted on ballot measures; no week-by-week rate can be read from that), how long any step takes a person, whether any caption stamp lands on the same second of the archived video, and anything on meetings beyond the sampled eleven, which `run.py` reads when it has network (see below).

## How it works

1. **Funded pairs.** A pair is a donor family with Schedule A money in a committee of an official (the funding map's `family_rows` and committees): 62. Money is per committee and per filing; a row repeated in another filing, or a 24-hour report repeating a Schedule A row, is listed and not counted; totals are never added across committees; outside spending (form 496) is shown apart. 60 of the 62 reached a committee that is the official's own, for another office, or a measure committee carrying the official's name.
2. **The donor's subject.** Each donor statement (its own words, or for six donors a press report kept apart) is read into `anchor` terms (a measure's name, a phrase particular to the proposal) and `topic` terms. A passage that hits an anchor is a *specific* candidate; one that hits only topic terms is a *topic* candidate.
3. **Speaker credit from the text.** A member's title and name after a `>>`, a sentence end, 'NEXT UP' or 'WE HAVE', and not a roll-call answer, open a turn (`SUPERVISOR X`, `CHAIR X`, `VICE CHAIR X`, with the surname spellings the captions are known to produce); the turn closes at the next such cue, a roll call, a clerk call, 'WITHOUT OBJECTION' or a speaker introducing themselves, or after 700 words. The Mayor's opening remarks are one turn, from the presiding officer's question to 'THANK YOU, MAYOR'. Doubt is **low** (surname spelled as the roster has it, a cue closes the turn, at most one `>>` between cue and passage, within 400 words), **medium** (a garbled spelling, no closing cue, a few `>>`, up to 700 words, any Chan or Chen without a district cue) or **high**.
4. **Minutes.** The minutes text is parsed for who was present, each vote (Ayes and Noes by surname, with its motion), motions, sponsors and the minutes' own summaries of who addressed the Board. A credit is `confirmed`, `consistent`, `conflict`, `silent` or `no_minutes` (definitions in `results.md`).
5. **Review.** A person's reading decides: accept (same measure or specific proposal), loose (same broad subject), reject (chance, or not the credited official's words), and whether the credit stands.
6. **Pairings.** From the accepted passages, one per funded pair, no two sharing a quote, ranked by the reviewer's preference, specific hits, the donor's own words, credit doubt and minutes.

## Q1. How often does the official say something on the donor's subject?

In the 11 sampled meetings (nine Board and committee meetings in July 2026, plus Board meetings on 6 January and 21 April), for the 60 funded pairs with a tied committee: **8 pairs (13%) have a fitting remark; 13 (22%) have a fitting or a broad one; 19 (32%) had any passage hit the terms; 41 (68%) had none.** The 8: Mahmood and Govern For California; Wong and the Police Officers Association; Walton and SEIU 1021; Walton and the Laborers; Walton and the Operating Engineers (the last two on the Mayor's say-so, not the unions' own statements); Wong and the Apartment Association, and Melgar and the Apartment Association (reported); Fielder and IFPTE Local 21 (reported). Every funded pair without a fitting remark is in `results.md` with the reason (47: no turn credited, no hit, or only chance hits). Reasons by count: no passage in the words credited to the official hit the terms (most); Mandelman has no credited turn (7 pairs, the Board President); every hit rejected as chance.

What the rate depends on, as read from the sample: the meetings (July's Board meetings voted on the November ballot measures, so measure-named donors fit then; the January and April meetings produced no accepted passage), the donor (a named measure gives a hit; a general mission statement, such as BOMA's or the Realtors', gave none), and the credit rule's reach (a quarter of caption words). So the rate above is a floor for the sampled meetings, not an estimate for all meetings.

**Pairings a week:** not estimable from these data. Four of five pairings come from the Board meeting of 14 July, the vote that placed measures on the ballot. The `meetings_read` and `unique_passages` rows of `results.md` give the figures for however many meetings a run reads; reading more weeks (a run with network does) is what would show a weekly spread.

## Q2. How is each quote credited, and how often do the minutes confirm or contradict it?

Credit is the text rule above, set against the reviewer's reading of the words around the passage and against the minutes. Rule doubt for the 34 distinct passages: 14 low, 17 medium, 3 high; the reviewer agreed with 29, disagreed with 2 and was unsure of 3 (see the bullet above). Disagreements were at medium doubt (a turn that did not close, `>>` marks inside it); the 3 high-doubt passages are one speech of Walton's that the reviewer agreed was his, from words inside it ('DISTRICT TEN RESIDENTS', thanks to named colleagues) and the chair's closing words, so a high doubt is a reason to look, not a finding of error. Minutes: see the bullet above; 10 of the 11 meetings' minutes were not read, so the count of contradictions (none) rests on 24 passages from one meeting. **Whether the site shows the speaker as found or marks it for review:** these data say low-doubt credit was not contradicted in 14 passages and medium doubt was wrong in 2 of 17 (12%), so the medium and high tiers need a person's check before they appear; low doubt could be shown with the cue's words beside it. The confirmation the minutes give is mostly of votes and motions, not of who spoke.

Limits the rule has, observed: it needs a title and name (a caption that gives only a garbled first name, as Mahmood's 15 July turn does, is missed); the President and the Mayor's answers are not credited; and in a committee a chair's introduction of a presenter can run into the presenter's words unless the presenter says who they are.

## Q3. Which steps needed a person's judgement, and how long did each take?

The table in `results.md` (section 3) lists nine steps with who did each, how many items, and the machine seconds (the whole offline run takes under a second per step). Judgement steps, in the order a reporter would meet them: (1) recognising a giver as the donor (done in the funding map: 17 families), (2) turning the donor's words into search terms (19 subjects), (3) deciding fit (45 passages: 16 accept, 10 loose, 19 reject), (4) agreeing or not with the credit (45), (5) reading minutes conflicts or silence (none conflicted here), (6) cutting the quote (20), (7) writing the reading (16), and (8) watching the video at the stamp, which **nobody did**. **No step was timed** and the reviewer was not a reporter, so the question "how long did each take" is not answered; the key result that waits on timing two or three reporters is not met. For the review screen: what the data say a reviewer must decide per pairing is the fit (accept, loose or reject), the speaker credit when the doubt is medium or high, the boundaries of the quote, and whether the minutes' silence or conflict matters.

## How this serves the objectives

- *Everything shown is current and correct:* every quote is cut from the transcript text by the program and checked word for word; money is stamped with the dataset's load date and retrieval date; the donor's words carry link, page date and retrieval date. Not checked: whether any stamp lands on the same second of the archived video, and the captions' own errors (names, numbers), which only the recording settles.
- *Let a reporter quickly understand pay-to-play speech:* `pairings.md` lays each lead out in one screen: words, credit, the donor's words, the money by committee, what the two have in common and where they differ, and how to check it. No reporter was timed.
- *Only surface things that are meaningful:* the chance rate above is the first run of the matching over real quotes and donors: 19 of 45 hits were chance, none of the 18 broad-topic hits fit, and 16 of 27 specific hits did. What counts as a match that stands out, as far as this sample goes: a hit on a measure's name or a phrase particular to the proposal, in words credited at low or medium doubt, from a donor with its own statement. That is a reading of one sample by one reviewer.

## How well sourced

- Transcripts: the 11 caption transcripts of the earlier hops (Legistar `Transcript.aspx?ID1=` pages, retrieved 2026-10-29), unchanged. Quotes are cut from them by the program.
- Minutes: the draft minutes of the 14 July 2026 Board meeting, <https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/bag071426_minutes_w5I3QUe.pdf> (printed 4:45 pm on 7/15/26), read in full and typed in excerpt into `data/minutes/52849.txt`; the parser was built and tested on that format. The minutes are action minutes and 'Draft'. The other 10 meetings' minutes were not read; `run.py` fetches them (through `pypdf`, pinned to 6.16.0, the latest release seen on a release list dated 2026-08-13; a newer one may exist (unverified)).
- Money and donors' statements: as collected by the funding map on 2026-10-30; not re-fetched here. The trimmed money snapshot is a copy.
- The Legistar calendar reading in `legistar.py` (form fields, row links, a fallback to the Granicus feeds, whose clip ids equal the transcript ids for meeting 52849) was written from the page's known form and the feed format seen in a search result; it has not been exercised against the live site. Whether it works is shown by the first section of `results.md` after a run with network.

## Open questions

**Looked for and not found**
- Meetings beyond the sampled eleven: none is read in the committed `results.md`, which is from an offline run; the program that reads them is in place. Where to look: `results.md`, section "Meetings read" (after a live run). Which committees and which months it reads is set by `FF_YEAR`, `FF_MAX_NEW_MEETINGS`.
- Minutes for 10 of the 11 sampled meetings: not read (offline); the minutes check has therefore been exercised on one meeting. Where it would show: `results.md` after a live run.
- A transcript-to-video offset: whether a stamp lands on the same second in the archived recording. Nothing read says; no recording was played.
- The Mayor's answers in the monthly appearance: not credited by the rule (the questioner's cue does not name who answers); only his opening remarks are.
- A time for any step: not measured.

**Claims not verified**
- That the Housing Trust Fund charter amendment is the Prop C of the donors' pages: the donors' pages (Govern For California: 'Prop C, Affordable Housing Trust Fund'; GrowSF: 'Prop C ... grows the Housing Trust Fund') and the minutes' item (File 260537) agree, but the ballot letters were not read from the Department of Elections (unverified).
- That the Apartment Association's $211,000 went to a committee that backs Prop C: it is a reporter's statement in The Real Deal of 29 October 2026, not the association's (as recorded in the funding map's reported priorities); the extract does not name the committee (unverified).
- That Walton is the speaker in pairing 3 (rule doubt high): the cue is three minutes earlier; the reviewer's agreement rests on 'DISTRICT TEN', thanks to named colleagues and the chair's close (my reading).
- That the minutes excerpt matches the PDF in every character: typed by hand from the page as fetched (unverified).
